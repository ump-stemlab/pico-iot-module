📜 The one rule

Do not use AI to write your code. Not for this module. Not the guided code, not the exercises, not the bits you are stuck on.

This is not about cheating. It is about which skills end up in your hands, and which ones end up nowhere.

Nothing in this module is hard enough to need help writing. It is all small enough to type, break, and fix yourself. A chatbot can hand you Activity 5, but it cannot hand you the project at the end, because nobody has written that one down yet.

Typing it yourself is not the slow way round It is the whole point of the next eleven activities.

When you are stuck, do this in order

1

Read the error

It names the line. Go and look at that line, and the one above it.

2

Read your code out loud

Slowly. Colons, brackets, capital letters, indentation. It is nearly always one of those.

3

Compare it with the picture

The page has the code as an image. Put them side by side, line by line.

4

Try it in Wokwi

Break the program into two lines and run those. Find out which half is wrong.

5

Ask your neighbour

Explaining it out loud fixes it about half the time, before they even answer.

6

Ask the instructor

Always available. Never the last resort you were afraid to use.

Get the same tiny program running in two places: a simulator in your browser, and a real chip on your desk.

There are eleven activities after this one. Each adds exactly one new idea. Today is the exception: everybody needs the same two tools working before Activity 1 makes any sense.

Self-paced means

After today, you set the pace — with your own hands on the keyboard.

💻 What is a Raspberry Pi Pico?

A computer with no screen, no keyboard and one job.

It runs one program

The instant it gets power, it starts that program. Nothing to click.

It talks through pins

Not through a screen. Each pin has a number, and your code refers to it by that number.

GP means General Purpose

Every pin you can control is GP-something. That is the number you type.

3.3 volts, everywhere

Five volts on a GP pin is the one thing that actually destroys a board.

It is not a small laptop Knowing how it is different explains most of what follows.

The board you will actually hold

For most activities you will not wire anything at all. The lights, the buttons, the screen and the sensors are already connected. Your job is the code.

The exceptions are Activity 3 (a servo) and Activity 11 (a soil sensor), which use the 40-pin header. Pin names on that header are not the same as GP numbers — the activity pages tell you which is which.

💬 The language: MicroPython

Python, cut down to fit on a chip the size of a fingernail.

What it is
PythonThe same language used for data science, websites and machine learning. What you learn here is not a toy dialect.
MicroA smaller version that fits in the Pico's memory. Same rules, same words, fewer libraries.
Why it suits youIt reads close to English, indentation does the work that brackets do elsewhere, and errors are written in plain sentences.

Two ways to run it

In a program

You write a file, press Run, and it does the whole thing from the top. That is what you will do in every activity.

In the Shell

You type one line after the >>> arrows and it happens immediately. Good for trying something out.

Three things that will catch you out A colon at the end of while, if and else lines; indentation — four spaces, and it has to line up; capital letters matter: Pin is not pin, and True is not true.

Step 5 — Parts, wires, and keeping your work

You will not need this until Activity 1, but know where it is.

  • Adding a part: press the blue + above the board and pick from the list — LED, pushbutton, servo, screen. It drops in unwired.
  • Wiring it: click a pin on the Pico, then click a pin on the part. A wire appears. Click a wire to change its colour or delete it.
  • The shortcut: most activities give you a diagram.json. Open that tab, select everything, paste theirs in. Every part appears, already wired.
  • Saving: Wokwi forgets everything the moment you close the tab, unless you save. Sign in, press Ctrl+S, and keep the link somewhere you will find it.

Step 3 — Plug the Pico in, then change one setting

Connect the Pico to your computer with the USB cable, and make sure the board's power switch SW5 is on. Look at the bottom-right corner of Thonny and choose MicroPython (Raspberry Pi Pico).

When >>> appears in the Shell, Thonny has found your Pico.

If Raspberry Pi Pico is not in the list Check the cable. Some USB cables only carry power, and they look identical to the ones that carry data.

Step 4 — Run it, then save it onto the board

Same five lines as Wokwi. Type them again — yes, really.

Code is shown as a picture — turn on JavaScript to see it.

Press Run. The Shell fills up, one line a second. Press Stop before you unplug anything.

Save it onto the Pico as main.py with Ctrl+S, choosing Raspberry Pi Pico as the location.

Three safety rules, every time Never connect a 5V pin to a GP pin — they are 3.3 V only. Never join a power pin to a GND pin. Switch the board off at SW5 before changing any wires.

🛠️ When it goes red

This will happen today. It is supposed to.

Read it from the bottom up The last line says what kind of mistake. The line above it says where. Then go and look — not at the whole program, at that line and the one above it.

Six first-day problems

What you seeWhy it happensFix
NameErrorPython does not recognise a word. Usually a lowercase pin instead of Pin, or true instead of True.Check capital letters.
SyntaxErrorMissing colon, bracket or quote.Look at the line number and count brackets/quotes.
IndentationErrorThe lines under while True: are not indented, or indented by different amounts.Make them all four spaces in.
Nothing printsYou pressed Run, but the Serial Monitor is closed or the wrong interpreter is selected.Open the Serial Monitor in Wokwi, or set Thonny to MicroPython (Raspberry Pi Pico).
Thonny cannot find the PicoThe cable only carries power, or SW5 is off.Try a different cable; check the power switch.
The program stops when you unplug the boardYou ran it from Thonny but did not save it onto the Pico.Save as main.py on the Pico, not on the computer.
None of these are your fault And none of them break anything. They are the normal texture of writing code.

📖 Words to remember

PicoThe Raspberry Pi Pico: a tiny computer that runs one program and talks to the world through pins.
GPIO / GP numberGeneral Purpose Input/Output. The number your code uses for a pin.
MicroPythonA small version of Python that fits on the Pico.
ProgramA file of instructions that the Pico runs from top to bottom.
ShellThe place you type one line at a time and see the result immediately.
SimulatorWokwi: a pretend Pico in a browser, where mistakes are free.
IndentationSpaces at the start of a line that show which lines belong inside a loop or decision.
Syntax errorA mistake in the grammar of the code, like a missing colon or bracket.
