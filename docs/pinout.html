Pin reference

Everything on the board is wired to a GP number on the Pico. Your code has to use that number. Keep this page open while you work.

Board labels are not pin numbers The picture printed on the board uses its own labels, like SW1(11) or SW4(37). Those are positions on the connector. In code you always use the GP number.

📡 Sensor bus (I²C)

SDA (GP0) and SCL (GP1) are two shared wires that every sensor talks on, like a class where everyone takes turns. Each sensor answers to its own number, called its address:

SensorAddress
OLED screen0x78
Temperature, humidity, pressure0x76
Accelerometer and gyroscope0x68
Compass0x7C
Proximity and light0x60
Air quality (TVOC)0x1A
Analogue-to-digital converter0x48
The fussy one The air quality sensor's clock must stay below 15 kHz.

⚡ Power pins

3V33.3 volts — what almost everything on this board runs on.
5VStronger; used by the buzzer and the USB supply.
GNDGround, the return path. Every circuit needs a GND connection or nothing works.

🎲 Reading analogue values

The four analogue inputs on connector H3 (gas sensor, battery level and two spares) do not go to the Pico directly. They pass through a converter chip and come back over the sensor bus — so reading a voltage on this board is an I²C job, not an ADC() job.

🧿 How to hold the board

  • The black 40-pin header sits along the top edge and the USB sockets are on the left.
  • Pin 1 is at the left-hand end of the header. The board has a small printed dot next to it.
  • Odd pins 1, 3, 5 … run along the lower row, nearest the LEDs and screen.
  • Even pins 2, 4, 6 … run along the upper row, nearest the edge of the board.

⚠️ Three safety rules

  1. Never connect a 5V pin to a GP pin. The GP pins are 3.3 V only.
  2. Never join a 5V pin straight to a 3V3 pin, and never join any power pin to a GND pin.
  3. Switch the board off at SW5 before changing any wires.
