Activity 8 — teacher notes

Internet and Data. One idea in two halves: the board joins a network, and it hands a message to a server whose job is passing messages on.

▶ Student page 📚 All teacher notes ← Activity 7 notes 📌 Pin reference
Keep this link to yourself. Unlisted and hidden from search engines, but the repository is public — anyone with the URL can read it.

What is being taught

  • One idea, two halves. Joining a network, and publishing to a broker. Neither is any use alone, and the second is the one they will remember. Do not split them.
  • This is the first activity that needs the Pico W and the first that can fail for reasons outside the program. Budget time for the network, not for the code.
  • New hardware, still no new pins. The weather sensor is the third part on GP0 and GP1, at 0x76. Say it out loud again; it is the second lesson in a row where the bus pays for itself.
  • The reading arrives as words with a unit on the end. Taking the unit off is the only genuinely new syntax, and it is the sibling of Activity 7’s reshaping step — that one was for a person, this one is for a machine.
  • while not is the quiet win. First loop in the module that stops by itself, and it reuses not from Activity 5 for something that finally matters.
  • Setting up goes outside the loop. Joining the WiFi every five seconds is the architectural mistake of the lesson and it half-works, which makes it worse.
  • The rate limit is a teaching point, not a footnote. Thirty a minute; going over looks exactly like a broken board.
  • No elif, no for loops, still. Nothing here needs either.

The two things that decide whether this lesson works

1. The network

  • Test it yourself, on a Pico W, in the room, before the lesson. Nothing else on this page matters if this fails.
  • A Pico W joins 2.4 GHz only. A dual-band router advertising one name can hand it a 5 GHz-only association and it will simply never join.
  • Captive portals are fatal. If the school network shows a sign-in page, no Pico will ever get past it — there is no browser on the board.
  • Phone hotspot is the agreed fallback for this module. Set it to 2.4 GHz (Android: Maximise compatibility; iPhone: Maximise Compatibility). One hotspot will take roughly eight to ten boards; plan a hotspot per group of three or four rather than one for the room.
  • Have the network name and password on the board (the whiteboard) before they start. Typing a password wrong is the single commonest cause of “it never joins”.
  • The Wokwi route needs no local network at all — only that wokwi.com and io.adafruit.com are reachable from the classroom browsers. Worth checking the school does not block either.

2. The accounts

  • One free account each, made in the lesson — Kamil’s call. Budget ten minutes and expect email-confirmation friction.
  • Everyone’s own account means everyone’s own 30 messages a minute, which is the main reason not to share one.
  • If accounts cannot be made, one shared account works, but then every board must use a different name for itself in the broker line, and the whole room shares one rate limit — push the wait up to fifteen seconds.
  • The key is a password. Say it once, firmly, at the start. Somebody will screenshot their screen for a friend. The My Key page can throw a leaked key away and issue a new one.
  • Feeds are private to an account by default. The page says so, and also says not to publish anything about a person.
  • The Adafruit IO screens change. The student page carries ten real screenshots, taken from Adafruit IO and Thonny for the STEM Lab’s own deck and now in docs/img/. Click through them yourself the week before; if a menu has moved, the words on the buttons will not have, and the page says so. Tell the STEM Lab so the pictures can be retaken.
  • The key is blacked out in the screenshot, deliberately, and the caption says so. Point at it when you give the security line — it is a better demonstration than the sentence.

The Wokwi route — what was actually verified

  • Wokwi’s simulated network reaches the real internet. Verified during this build, in the browser: a simulated Pico W joined Wokwi-GUEST, opened a connection to the real io.adafruit.com, and was refused with a genuine MQTTException(5,) when handed a deliberately fake key. Nothing was faked by the simulator.
  • Earlier notes in this repository said WiFi and MQTT were real-board-only. That was true once and is no longer; CONTEXT.md §5.2 has been corrected.
  • The starter project carries a custom BME280 chip written by the STEM Lab — Wokwi has no built-in BME280 — plus a screen, and bme280.py, ssd1306.py and umqtt_simple.py as project files. main.py is empty on purpose.
  • Verified on the rewired project: i2c.scan() answered [60, 118] with both parts on GP0/GP1, and the sensor returned ('21.64C', '1010.39hPa', '55.04%').
  • The exact probe, for anyone who wants to repeat it:
    Code is shown as a picture — turn on JavaScript to see it.
  • The chip’s sliders are the whole route. Press play, then click the green block: temperature, pressure and humidity sliders appear. Not discoverable. Demonstrate it on the projector.
  • Wokwi cannot create a folder on the simulated Pico, which is why the broker library is a flat umqtt_simple.py there and umqtt.simple on the real board.

Exercise answer — the reading on the screen as well as on the graph

Code is shown as a picture — turn on JavaScript to see it.
  • The reading is taken once and used twice. That is the point of the exercise. Taking it twice is not wrong enough to fail, but the two will disagree and it looks like a bug.
  • The screen gets the whole reading, the graph gets the stripped one. Accept the stripped version on the screen too — but ask which one a person would rather read.
  • Nothing above the loop changes except two lines: the screen’s import, and the screen itself after the bus. Watch for the screen being made before the bus line.
  • Wipe inside the loop, show last. Activity 6’s order, unchanged, and it still catches people two activities later.
  • Down-numbers 0 and 16, as in Activities 6 and 7. Accept 0 and anything from 12 to 40.
  • Accept any order of publish and screen within the round. Publishing first is marginally better because the network is the slow part, but do not make a thing of it.
  • The most instructive wrong answer is putting the screen work outside the loop: one reading appears, freezes, and the graph carries on without it.
Wokwi pairs: the screen is already in the starter project So is ssd1306.py. Nobody needs to add a part or a file for this exercise on either route — if a group is trying to, they have started a new project.

Before the lesson

  • Run the whole thing yourself, on the network the class will use. Both routes. This is the one activity where the room can defeat a correct program.
  • Put bme280.py and umqtt.simple on every real board yourself. Same argument as Activities 6 and 7 — the library step is not what today is about, and the package manager needs the board connected and idle.
  • Files from ump-stemlab/stemcube on a shared drive or a USB stick. Assume the lab has no internet for GitHub.
  • Make your own Adafruit IO account and dashboard first, and have it on the projector before anybody starts. Seeing a graph move is what makes the room want one.
  • Check the school does not block io.adafruit.com or wokwi.com.
  • Decide the network answer in advance: school WiFi, or hotspots. If hotspots, tell students the day before so the phones are charged.
  • Have the Wokwi starter link ready to paste into a chat or write on the board.
  • Thonny preset to MicroPython (Raspberry Pi Pico); pin reference open on every machine.
  • If a board misbehaves, scan the bus before believing anything else:

118 is the weather sensor, 60 the screen, 104 the tilt sensor. On a real LilEx5 the list is longer — every I²C part answers.

Teaching notes

  • Open with your own dashboard on the projector and breathe on the sensor. The graph twitching because somebody in the room breathed is the hook, and it takes fifteen seconds.
  • Do the broker with three volunteers. One publishes a number on a sticky note to a person in the middle; the other two ask that person for it. Nobody in the room forgets what a broker is after that.
  • The publishing widget on the student page is worth five minutes. Publish once with the unit left on, change the username and watch the address change, then publish thirty times. Three failures, nothing typed.
  • Name the two halves separately as they happen. “We are on the network” is one milestone; “we are talking to the broker” is another. Students who cannot tell them apart cannot debug either.
  • Let them see the second between the Shell line and the graph point. That second is the internet, and it is the only time in this module they can watch it happen.
  • Say “still no new pins” and point at the pin table. Twice in two lessons.
  • The security line is short and firm: the key is a password. One sentence, not a lecture, and repeated once at the end.
  • Safety rules unchanged: never 5V to a GP pin, never a power pin to GND, power off at SW5 before changing wires.
  • Next lesson is soil moisture — an analogue pin, and the discovery that a sensor’s numbers mean nothing until you have measured what they mean on your own sensor. The board listens to this same broker in Activity 11, so today’s middleman is not spent.
