Each activity adds exactly one idea and re-teaches nothing. If a class is struggling, the fix is almost always in the previous activity rather than the current one.

How the module is meant to run

Wokwi first, then the board

On the LilEx5 the wiring is inside the PCB, so the simulator is the only place students see a circuit. Both routes are on every page as tabs.

Code cannot be copied

Every program is drawn as a picture and the clipboard is guarded. Students type, or they learn nothing. This is deliberate — do not paste code into a chat for them.

No elif before Activity 9

Kept out of Activities 1–8 so the two-road picture of a decision survives. It arrives in Activity 9, where three zones is the first honest reason for it, and is fair game after that.

Libraries may be pasted

The no-copying rule is about students’ own programs. From Activity 7 a library file is pasted or copied onto the Pico — nobody retypes one. Say so, or the rule looks arbitrary.

No for loops, still

Every repeat in this module is a forever-loop, and nothing up to Activity 11 has needed anything else.

Answers live here, not there

Student pages carry the task, the expected result and progressive clues. The worked answer is on these pages only.

Three safety rules, every lesson Never connect a 5V pin to a GP pin — GP pins are 3.3 V only. Never join a power pin to GND. Switch the board off at SW5 before changing wires.

Activities 8, 10 and 11 add no pins at all — the tilt sensor and the weather sensor both joined the bus, and the Pico W’s WiFi radio is inside the chip rather than on a pin. Activity 9 adds GP26, the module’s first analogue pin and its first wire to the 40-pin header; only GP26, GP27 and GP28 can measure a voltage, and the H3 connector’s analogue inputs are not these — they come back over I²C. SW5 is the power switch and is not on a GP pin. GP0 and GP1 are the bus — shared by the screen and all eight I²C sensors, so nothing else may ever be wired to them. Full map: pin reference.

Kit check, once a term

  • Every board: SW1, SW2 and SW3 click; LED1, LED2 and LED3 light; the buzzer sounds; the OLED screen shows something when the Activity 7 hello program is run on it; the accelerometer answers when the Activity 8 tilt program is run on it.
  • ssd1306.py, imu.py and vector3d.py already on every real Pico, and on a shared drive or USB stick for the Wokwi route — see the Activity 7 notes and the Activity 8 notes.
  • Thonny installed, interpreter preset to MicroPython (Raspberry Pi Pico).
  • One USB cable per board that carries data, not only power.
  • Soil probes: test every one on a known cup before the lesson — a corroded probe reads exactly like a wiring fault. Store them rinsed and dry, never standing in wet soil, with their calibration numbers on a sticky note. Three female-to-female jumpers per probe, and a cup of soil per pair. See the Activity 9 notes.
  • Wokwi reachable from the school network, and the pin reference bookmarked on every machine.
